The moss under Maya’s sneakers felt like plush green carpet, damp and quiet. 
She had crossed the rickety wooden bridge behind her garden a hundred times, but today, she kept walking.

The forest didn't dark-out all at once. It softened.
 Sunlight filtered through the pine canopy in long,
 slanted beams, turning speckles of floating dust into suspended gold. 
Every sound felt amplified—the crisp snap of a twig, the rhythmic drumming of a distant woodpecker, 
the whispering hush of wind through high oak leaves.

A flash of robin’s-egg blue caught her eye near the base of a hollowed-out birch.
 Curled inside the knot of the trunk was a glass marble, its center swirled with silver thread. 
Maya knelt, her small hands brushing aside damp earth to scoop it up. 
It was cold, smooth, and surprisingly heavy.

"Looking for the rest?"

Maya started, dropping the marble into her pocket. 
A few yards away sat a red fox on a fallen log, watching her with dark, intelligent eyes.
 It didn't run. It simply tilted its head, stretched its front legs, 
and trotted deeper into the brush, stopping once to look back over its shoulder.

Heart thumping with a mix of fear and electric curiosity, Maya followed. 
The trees grew taller, their roots twisting above the soil like giant wooden veins. 
The air smelled of sap, wet stone, and wild berry bushes.

They reached a tiny clearing where a creek bubbled over smooth gray river stones.
 Scattered along the bank were dozens of similar glass marbles, catching the noon sun like dropped stars.

Maya smiled, sinking onto her knees beside the stream.
The forest wasn't a place of monsters or darkness; it was a giant, waiting secret. 
She tucked a second marble into her pocket and began to build a small stone tower for the fox.
