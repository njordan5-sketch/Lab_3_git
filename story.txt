The moss under Maya’s sneakers felt like plush green carpet, damp and quiet. 
She had crossed the rickety wooden bridge behind her garden a hundred times, but today, she kept walking.

The forest didn't dark-out all at once. It softened.
 Sunlight filtered through the pine canopy in long,
 slanted beams, turning speckles of floating dust into suspended gold. 
Every sound felt amplified—the crisp snap of a twig, the rhythmic drumming of a distant woodpecker, 
the whispering hush of wind through high oak leaves.

A flash of robin’s-egg blue caught her eye near the base of a hollowed-out birch.
 Curled inside the knot of the trunk was a glass marble, its center swirled with silver thread. 
Maya knelt, her small hands brushing aside damp earth to scoop it up. 
It was cold, smooth, and surprisingly heavy.

She turned the glass sphere over in her palm, admiring the tiny, spiraling galaxy locked inside it. But as the sun caught the marble at just the right angle, the silver swirl began to pulse with a low, blue light.

Before Maya could drop it, the ground beneath her sneakers vanished.

The damp earth dissolved into nothingness, and Maya tumbled straight through the forest floor. She braced for a painful crash, but instead of hit dirt, she plunged through a sea of glowing, violet clouds. Gravity flipped upside down. The rushing wind smelled of cinnamon and ozone rather than damp pine and moss.

She landed with a soft thud on a hill of shimmering, obsidian sand. Skyward, two crescent moons hung over a sprawling landscape of towering glass spires and rivers that flowed uphill with liquid starlight. Standing at the base of the hill was the same robin's-egg blue marble—except now, it was as tall as a house, glowing like a beacon in this silent, foreign world.
